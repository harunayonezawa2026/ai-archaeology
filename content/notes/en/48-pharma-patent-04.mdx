## Conclusion first

On November 22, 1951, three researchers at the private Mexican research firm **Syntex SA** in Mexico City—Carl Djerassi (Austrian-born chemist), Luis E. Miramontes (Mexican chemistry student), and George Rosenkranz (Hungarian-born, Syntex's head of research)—established the priority date for U.S. Patent US2744122A. The subject was **17α-ethynyl-19-nortestosterone**, later named norethindrone (U.S.) / norethisterone (international nonproprietary name)—**the first synthetic progestin to retain progestational activity by oral administration**, a milestone in pharmaceutical history.

Why this invention can be read as "the prehistory to modern oral contraceptives, hormone replacement therapy, and assisted reproductive technology" rests on four points. (1) Russell Marker established a mass-synthesis path from diosgenin in Mexican wild yam (*Dioscorea*) to progesterone in 1944 (the Marker degradation), which created Syntex's research foundation. (2) Djerassi and colleagues combined two molecular modifications—deletion of the 19-methyl (19-nor) and introduction of a 17α-ethynyl group (C≡CH)—to obtain **oral activity that resists hepatic first-pass metabolism**. This was a molecular-design answer to the era's central problem: "natural hormones don't work orally." (3) The patent's priority date 1951-11-22 is consistent with Miramontes's lab notebook entry of 1951-10-15 (held at UNAM), 35 days earlier. (4) Frank Colton at Searle synthesized norethynodrel (US2725389A) in 1953, and Pincus / Rock's team obtained FDA approval for Enovid as a menstrual-disorder treatment in 1957 and as an oral contraceptive in 1960. Norethindrone-based products (Ortho-Novum etc.) followed.

The DB row PH-008 in `~/ai-archaeology/db/candidates.tsv` lists "Syntex (Mexico City), Carl Djerassi/Luis Miramontes/George Rosenkranz, 1951 synthesis, 1956 grant," consistent with the primary source on Google Patents. Unlike the consecutive errors in Days 8/9/10/11 (inventor names, patent numbers, priority dates), this case shows **DB-agreement**, the second such confirmed case after Day 11's aspirin US644077A. On the narrative side, however, the **Miramontes performed the synthesis but Djerassi gets the front of the story** credit problem is widely discussed; we need to separate the objective patent fact (three co-inventors) from the narrative bias (Djerassi-centric).

This article excavates: (1) Claim 1 verbatim from US2744122A as obtained on Google Patents and decoding of the six-step synthesis, (2) the path from Russell Marker's mass progesterone synthesis from Mexican yam (Marker degradation) through the founding of Syntex, (3) consistency between Miramontes's lab notebook 1951-10-15 and the priority date 1951-11-22, (4) the inventor credit problem, (5) parallel running of Searle's Frank Colton norethynodrel US2725389A, and (6) the 70-year reckoning to modern oral contraceptives, hormone replacement therapy, and ART.

## 2. The identity of the topic (Claude summary)

```
[Prompt 1]
Summarize the path by which Djerassi / Miramontes / Rosenkranz at Mexican Syntex SA
arrived at 17α-ethynyl-19-nortestosterone (norethindrone) in three steps:
(1) Russell Marker's mass synthesis of progesterone from Mexican yam diosgenin,
(2) molecular design aiming at oral activity by 19-nor modification of progesterone,
(3) acquisition of metabolic resistance through 17α-ethynyl introduction.
```

> **Step 1 (starting point)**: In 1944, Russell E. Marker, an organic chemist at Pennsylvania State University, established a five-step chemical conversion path to **progesterone (luteal hormone)** starting from the sapogenin **diosgenin** abundantly obtainable from wild yam *Dioscorea composita* (locally called *cabeza de negro*) in Veracruz, Mexico. This was later called the "**Marker degradation**" and could produce progesterone at **one ten-thousandth the price** of progesterone derived from European animal adrenal sources at the time. Marker moved to Mexico City the same year and co-founded Syntex SA with Emeric Somlo and Federico Lehmann. Marker left Syntex in 1949, but George Rosenkranz inherited and improved the Marker degradation, and Syntex became the world's largest steroid raw-material supplier.
>
> **Step 2 (19-nor modification)**: At the time, progesterone was effective only by injection; oral administration led to rapid inactivation by hepatic first-pass metabolism. Maximilian Ehrenstein had reported in 1944 that 19-nor modification (deletion of the 19-methyl group) of animal adrenal steroids could enhance oral activity. Djerassi joined Syntex from the University of Wisconsin in 1949 and designed a route in which Marker-degradation-derived estrone served as starting material: estrone lower alkyl ether → alkali metal reduction in liquid ammonia → mineral acid hydrolysis → chromic acid oxidation → 19-norprogesterone-series skeleton.
>
> **Step 3 (17α-ethynyl introduction)**: Inhoffen et al. had reported in 1938 that introducing a **17α-ethynyl group (−C≡CH)** at position 17 of estradiol significantly enhanced oral activity (ethynylestradiol, still the standard estrogen component of low-dose pills today). Djerassi and colleagues applied this 17α-ethynylation to the progesterone-series skeleton: with a 19-norprogesterone-series 3-enol ether, react with acetylene (HC≡CH) in the presence of an alkali metal alkoxide → final mineral acid hydrolysis → **17α-ethynyl-19-nortestosterone (norethindrone)**. Miramontes performed the final-step synthesis in the lab on October 15, 1951, and recorded it in his notebook (later held at UNAM).

```
[Prompt 2]
Read US2744122A Claim 1 and decode the six-step synthesis. Be clear about the
difference from the era's mainstream (injectable progesterone).
```

> **US2744122A Claim 1 verbatim**: "A process for the production of a compound having the following formula: [chemical structure] which comprises reducing a lower alkyl ether of estrone with an alkali metal in liquid ammonia followed by hydrolysis with a mineral acid and oxidation with chromic acid to form Δ-19-norandrosten-3,17-dione, selectively forming a 3-enol ether of said dione and treating said ether with acetylene in the presence of an alkali metal alkoxide, followed by hydrolysis with a mineral acid."
>
> Six synthesis steps: (a) **estrone lower alkyl ether** as starting material, (b) **alkali metal (Na/K) reduction in liquid ammonia** (Birch-reduction-like reduction of the aromatic A ring), (c) **mineral acid hydrolysis** to deprotect the 3-position enol ether, (d) **chromic acid oxidation** to obtain Δ-19-norandrosten-3,17-dione, (e) **selective formation of a 3-enol ether** (leaving the 17-ketone, enol-ethering only the 3-position), (f) **treatment with acetylene in the presence of an alkali metal alkoxide** (HC≡CH addition to the 17-ketone to construct 17α-ethynyl-17β-ol) → **mineral acid hydrolysis** to release the final enol ether and regenerate the 3-keto-Δ4 structure → norethindrone obtained.
>
> **Difference from the era's mainstream**: In the 1950s, progesterone hormone therapy was primarily (i) intramuscular injection of animal-adrenal- or placenta-derived progesterone (expensive and requiring frequent administration), (ii) 19-norprogesterone (slight oral activity, never commercialized). Norethindrone's pharmacological profile of **7–8x progesterone potency by oral administration** was achieved by combining (α) metabolic resistance from 19-nor modification and (β) protection of the 17-OH from metabolism by 17α-ethynylation. This patent is a "substance + process" patent typical of the U.S. patent system at the time.

## 4. The peculiarity of Mexico and Syntex SA at the time

In 1944, Russell Marker left Pennsylvania State University and harvested wild yam *Dioscorea composita* in Veracruz, Mexico, mass-synthesizing progesterone via Marker degradation. The same year, Marker co-founded Syntex SA in Mexico City with attorney Emeric Somlo and chemist Federico Lehmann. "Syn" is from synthesis, "tex" is from Mexico. Marker left Syntex in 1949 (taking detailed manufacturing processes with him and founding Botanica-Mex), but George Rosenkranz (Hungarian-born, trained at ETH Zurich under Leopold Ruzicka—who won the 1939 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for steroid/terpene research) inherited and improved Marker degradation, making Syntex the world's largest steroid raw-material supplier. Djerassi joined from Wisconsin in 1949.

Mexico's peculiarities are threefold: (a) **mass and low-cost supply of diosgenin** from wild *Dioscorea* stands, (b) research freedom away from U.S. and European patent systems, (c) concentration of Jewish and Eastern European scientists (Rosenkranz, Djerassi, etc.) who fled Nazism and the Cold War. Syntex of the 1950s symbolized "Mexico's biotechnology golden age" before relocating R&D to Palo Alto, U.S., in the 1960s and being acquired by Roche in 1994.

## 5. U.S. vs. Mexican patent system differences

US2744122A is a U.S. patent. A Mexican patent likely existed separately, but this article does not confirm. Mexico's patent system at the time was based on the 1928 "Ley de Propiedad Industrial," and substance patents on pharmaceuticals were not allowed until the 1991 reform (only process patents were obtainable for pharmaceuticals). Meanwhile, the U.S. allowed substance + process patents at the time, and Syntex prioritized U.S. market protection by filing a U.S. patent.

Syntex's strategy was "**manufacture in Mexico, sell in the U.S. and Europe**." Using Marker degradation to produce diosgenin-derived progesterone, cortisone intermediates, and estrogen intermediates at low cost, Syntex established a **raw-material supply business** licensed to U.S. and European pharmaceutical companies (Searle, Pfizer, Schering, etc.). In 1957, Syntex itself licensed norethindrone under the Ortho-Novum brand to Ortho Pharmaceutical (J&J subsidiary), and obtained FDA oral contraceptive approval in 1962.

## 6. Path from 1957 FDA menstrual-disorder approval to 1960 oral-contraceptive approval

In May 1957, the FDA approved Searle's norethynodrel (Enovid 10mg) as a **treatment for menstrual disorders and infertility**, not as an oral contraceptive. Pincus (Worcester Foundation for Experimental Biology) and Rock (Harvard Medical School) conducted clinical trials of norethynodrel in Río Piedras, Puerto Rico, etc., from 1956 to 1957, and applied to the FDA in 1959 for additional indication as an oral contraceptive. On May 9, 1960, the FDA officially approved Enovid as an **oral contraceptive**—the world's first formal approval of an oral contraceptive.

Syntex's norethindrone (Ortho-Novum, via Ortho Pharmaceutical) obtained FDA oral contraceptive approval in 1962 and continues to be used as the main component of low-dose pills (Loestrin, Necon, Modicon, etc.). Searle's norethynodrel lost prescription share in the 1980s and is no longer used. The reasons **norethindrone remained the main component of modern oral contraceptives** are (α) high oral bioavailability, (β) low androgenic activity, and (γ) compatibility with diverse dosing settings (low-dose pills, low-dose progestin-only pills).

## 7. Ethical issues in the Puerto Rico clinical trials

The Puerto Rico clinical trials conducted by Pincus / Rock from 1956 to 1957 are historically criticized for **lack of informed consent and concealment of side-effect reports**. Subjects were impoverished women in Río Piedras (many Spanish-only), and explanations of side effects (nausea, headache, dizziness, thrombosis) are said to have been insufficient. Three subject deaths were reported, but autopsies were not performed.

The ethical issues of these clinical trials are referenced as a "**reflective case**" in the norm-formation process of clinical trial ethics that follows: (a) the 1947 Nuremberg Code (reflection on Nazi medical experimentation), (b) the 1964 Helsinki Declaration, and (c) the 1979 Belmont Report. They can also be read as the prehistory of modern reproductive medicine / emerging-country clinical-trial ethics issues (e.g., the 2018 He Jiankui CRISPR human embryo clinical application in China).

## 8. Author's interpretation: the lineage of "designed pharmaceuticals"

Norethindrone US2744122A is an early example of a "**designed drug**." The four elements—(1) a target (oral activation of progestational activity), (2) known structure-activity relationships (Ehrenstein 1944 19-nor modification, Inhoffen 1938 17α-ethynyl modification), (3) rational selection of the starting material (Marker-degradation-derived estrone), and (4) design of a six-step synthesis—combined into **hypothesis-driven molecular design** with a different philosophy from the era's mainstream random screening (screening of natural extracts and microbial fermentation products).

The **rational drug design** of Squibb's Ondetti & Cushman captopril (1976), excavated on Day 11, came 25 years after norethindrone (1951) and applied the philosophy of "**target structure hypothesis → rational design of inhibitors**" to ACE inhibitors. The two are connected by a lineage that spans 50–70 years: (a) 1951 norethindrone (rational design of receptor agonists), (b) 1962 propranolol (ICI James Black's β-receptor antagonist exploration), (c) 1976 captopril (rational design of enzyme inhibitors), (d) 1990s HIV protease inhibitors / tyrosine kinase inhibitors, (e) 2010s and beyond AI-driven molecular design (AlphaFold / structure-based drug discovery / molecular generative AI)—a continuous **hypothesis-driven pharmaceutical design** lineage.

However, this lineage connection is the author's interpretation, and the molecular design methods, targets, and historical contexts of each case differ. Direct narrative linkage from "norethindrone → modern AI drug discovery" is appealing on the time-scale axis but technically separate. We read this at the level of "**overlap of problem-consciousness**."
